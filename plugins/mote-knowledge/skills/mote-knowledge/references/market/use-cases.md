# Use Cases

Concrete pictures of Mote in use: short scenarios that show the product solving a
real problem for a real person, grouped by who is using it. Useful for the example
in a blog post, the scenario on a landing page, or the "day in the life" of a case
study. These are illustrations, not an exhaustive list.

## Students

**Reaching grade-level text.** A student reading below grade level opens the
science article their class is studying. They turn on
[Read Aloud](../product/features/read-aloud.md) and follow the text highlighted word
by word, adjusting the voice and speed, taking part in the same lesson as everyone
else, rather than being handed an easier sheet.

**Taking part in a new language.** A multilingual learner opens a class webpage or
slide. They [translate](../product/features/webpage-translation.md) a passage into
their first language, listen to it read aloud, and look new words up in the
[Dictionary](../product/features/dictionary.md) with its picture and native-language
definition, contributing to the lesson now, while their English grows.

**Getting ideas down.** A student who finds spelling exhausting drafts in Writing
Mode: [Speech to Text](../product/features/speech-to-text.md) to focus on their
ideas rather than typing, then [Text Prediction](../product/features/text-prediction.md)
(set to a topic their teacher shared) to practice. With the draft down, they run
[Mote Writing Review](../product/writing-review.md), read why each issue is
flagged, and fix it themselves.

**Quietening an overwhelming page.** A student who loses focus on a dense page
turns on [Focus Mode](../product/features/focus-mode.md) to strip away the clutter,
and [Screen Mask](../product/features/screen-mask.md) to dim everything but the line
they are reading.

**Making a PDF workable.** A student is handed a PDF worksheet, the kind of file
other tools could never make accessible. One click sends it to
[Mote PDF](../product/mote-pdf.md), and the full toolkit applies: read-aloud (maths
included), the Dictionary, adding answers by voice or text, and annotation.

## Teachers

**Including the whole class.** A teacher sets one reading task for a class that
spans several reading levels. Because every student has the Chrome Extension, each reaches
the material their own way (by ear, in translation, with the screen masked)
without the teacher preparing thirty versions.

**Building accessible content.** A teacher generates a vocabulary list for a new
topic and shares it with the class; records voice instructions straight into a
Google Slide so students can work at their own pace; and toggles Read Aloud and
Translation into a Google Form so every student can complete a quiz independently.

**Building a language curriculum in Mote.** An English Learner department, rather than buying
supplemental curriculum, builds its own listening and speaking activities inside
Google Forms and Slides: Read Aloud and Translation make the material accessible, and
students respond by voice with Voice Recording or Speech to Text. The activities stay
aligned to the class content and reach every student in their normal Google Workspace
flow, so Mote extends, and in places replaces, the bought-in curriculum.

**Keeping tools in proportion.** A teacher can dial support up or down. For a
student becoming over-reliant on Translation, they switch it off for certain
classwork; before a test, Assessment Mode restricts features for the whole class for
a set time. The tools support learning; they are not a permanent crutch.

## Administrators

**Rolling out and seeing the impact.** An administrator assigns licenses by domain,
CSV, or email, and sets student and teacher roles. Through
[Mote Platform](../product/platform.md) they see which features are used and where,
and pull an impact report: a full PDF overview of usage across the institution.
