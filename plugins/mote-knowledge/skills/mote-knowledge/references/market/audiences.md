# Audiences

Mote is, first and foremost, a product for **students**, and it is sold mainly to
the **districts** that put it in their hands. This file maps who Mote serves to the
parts of Mote that help them, and how the audiences connect when a deal forms. The
feature files say *what a tool is*; this file says *who it is for*.

## Students Mote is built for

Mote is designed for students who face a barrier between themselves and their
learning. Three groups matter most.

**Students with learning disabilities,** including dyslexia, dysgraphia, and
attention-related needs, and students with IEPs or 504 plans. What helps:
[Read Aloud](../product/features/read-aloud.md) to access text by ear,
[Screen Mask](../product/features/screen-mask.md) and
[Focus Mode](../product/features/focus-mode.md) to cut visual load, and
[Text Prediction](../product/features/text-prediction.md) and
[Speech to Text](../product/features/speech-to-text.md) to get words onto the page.

**Students with reading and writing challenges,** students reading or writing below
grade level, without necessarily a formal diagnosis. What helps: Read Aloud to reach
text above their independent reading level,
[Mote Writing Review](../product/writing-review.md) to learn from their own
mistakes, Text Prediction, and the [Dictionary](../product/features/dictionary.md).

**Multilingual learners,** emerging bilinguals and English language learners. What
helps: [Webpage Translation](../product/features/webpage-translation.md) (which can
also read the translated text aloud), the Dictionary's native-language support, and
Read Aloud to connect written and spoken English.

## Teachers

Mote gives a teacher a realistic way to include every
student: because the support sits in the student's own hands, the teacher does not
have to differentiate every task by hand, or wait on scarce specialist staff.

## Administrators and districts: the buyer

Districts are Mote's buyer. A district leader weighs equitable access
at scale, alignment to [UDL](../education/udl.md) and [MTSS](../education/mtss.md),
privacy and compliance (FERPA, COPPA, GDPR), usage reporting, simple deployment, and
an affordable per-seat cost. [Mote Platform](../product/platform.md) is built
for exactly that.

## Who hears what

| Audience | What we say | Budget line |
|---|---|---|
| **Special education director, AT specialist** | One toolkit for every caseload, with the controls and usage data your team reviews | IDEA Part B; early intervening services |
| **English Learner or multilingual director** | Support to access grade-level content, matched to each student's English proficiency. As a student's English grows, you change the support: a newcomer might hear the text in their home language, while a student further along has Translation turned off and keeps the picture dictionary, so they work out complex words in English | Title III; IDEA for students with IEPs; Title I or general funds |
| **Curriculum, MTSS, digital learning** | One set of materials that each student can access according to their need, without building every lesson three times | Title I schoolwide; general funds |
| **IT director** | Deploy from the Google Admin console, sign in with Google or ClassLink, FERPA and COPPA compliant | Not the payer; removes the no |
| **Teachers** | Hand out one piece of content, and each student accesses it the way they need: read aloud, translated, or with word support. A student with dyslexia, a reluctant writer, a newcomer: each gets their tool from the same material | Not the payer; the champion |

## Objections and answers

These are the objections buyers raised on our calls, with answers the product
backs up. Where the honest answer is "not yet", we say so.

| They say | We answer |
|---|---|
| "People above us don't want all the kids to have access if they don't have an IEP or a 504" | Every student has the same tools, and admins and teachers decide who uses which: for the whole school, a class, or one student. Students with accommodations aren't singled out, because everyone has the same Mote Sidebar |
| "Teachers haven't read the accommodations list, or haven't set it up" | IT installs Mote for everyone from the Google Admin console, and admins set each tool once for the school. During a test, Assessment Mode can keep tools on for the students whose IEP requires them |
| "We already have Read&Write" | Here's how Mote fits the workflow you already have, how easy it is to roll out, and how its features match the ones your teachers use. Send us your Read&Write quote and we'll show you a side-by-side. Don't claim we're cheaper |
| "How do we show it's being used?" | Teachers see usage per student (Read Aloud minutes, translations). Admins see usage across the school, and the Impact Report summarizes it for the district |
| "Is student data safe?" | FERPA and COPPA compliant, Student Privacy Pledge signatory, data stored in the US |
| "My district won't pay for it" | Give us the email of the person who decides, and we'll make the case to them. Meanwhile, Unlimited is $99 a year for one teacher and gives up to 5 classes a 90-day trial |
| "Does it work on iPads?" | Mote for iOS brings Read Aloud, Translation and more to iPad. Don't promise the same tools as on Chromebooks; see the comparison in [mote-ios.md](../product/mote-ios.md) |
| "Does it work during tests?" | Read Aloud works in Google Forms quizzes, including in Locked Mode, and Assessment Mode controls which tools are on during a test. For locked-down exams, Mote for Assessments runs as a kiosk app on a dedicated Chromebook |

Don't claim that Mote works in lockdown browsers other than Google Forms Locked
Mode.
