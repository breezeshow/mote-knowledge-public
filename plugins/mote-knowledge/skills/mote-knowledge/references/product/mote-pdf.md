# Mote PDF

**Description.** Mote PDF is Mote's product for hosting PDFs and making them
accessible. The [Mote Chrome Extension](./chrome-extension.md) tools already work on *accessible* PDFs
on their own; Mote PDF goes further: it makes a hosted PDF fully workable with the
complete set of Mote Sidebar tools (Read Aloud, Translation, Dictionary, Highlighter,
Screen Mask, and the rest), and adds PDF-specific mark-up: highlighting, annotation,
and text boxes on the document itself.

**Accessibilizing scanned PDFs.** Mote PDF runs **OCR** on scanned and photocopied
PDFs (the ones where highlighting the text does nothing) and *accessibilizes*
them. Choose the document's language, and once it has run the whole toolkit works:
Play Page reads it aloud, the Dictionary looks up any word, Translation handles a
passage, and the Screen Mask cuts the glare. Students can highlight, annotate and
type straight onto the page, and it all **saves into the file**, so the work travels
with the document when they hand it in.

**How to use it.** Go to **app.mote.com/pdf** and drop a PDF in, or with a PDF open
and the Mote Chrome Extension open use the **export to Mote** button. In Google Drive, the
**Mote PDF Workspace app** opens a file in one click (or right-click a PDF and
choose **Open with Mote**), and from the Mote PDF web app you can browse and open
your Google Drive files directly. Edits **save back to Google Drive** with the
changes in them.

**Why it exists.** PDFs are everywhere in education (worksheets, articles,
textbooks) and they are often the *least* accessible content a student meets:
fixed, dense, and hard to interact with. Mote PDF turns a hosted PDF into something a
student can hear, translate, look words up in, and mark up, bringing the whole Mote
toolkit to a format that usually locks students out.

**Where to find it.** Via the **export to Mote** button when a PDF is open with the
Chrome Extension, or by right-clicking a PDF in Google Drive.
