# Embedded Voice Recorder

**Description.** The Embedded Voice Recorder puts Mote's recorder directly inside
the tools teachers and students already use. Look for the Mote icon in Google Docs,
Slides, Classroom, Forms, and Gmail to record and drop audio straight into your
content. **Mote for Google Slides** records or generates audio inside a
presentation; **Voice Recorder for Google Forms** lets students answer questions by
voice; **Mote for Gmail** adds a voice message to an email; and **Mote for Google
Classroom** shares voice messages with a whole class. Recordings can be **shared as
an embed code** for platforms like Wakelet and Canva, or **added as voice notes
anywhere**; the sharing method adapts to each platform (a link, an embed, or a
comment). Every recording can be **auto-transcribed**, and that transcript
translated into more than 60 languages.

**How to use it.** Open the platform you are working in and look for the Mote icon.
Click it to start recording, click again to stop, and the audio embeds into your
content. Sharing options (link, embed code, or voice comment) depend on the
platform.

**Why it exists.** Adding audio to classwork usually means breaking out of whatever
tool you're in. Mote's recorder lives *inside* Google Docs, Slides, Classroom,
Forms, and Gmail, so a teacher or student records right where they already are and
the audio embeds in place. And it doesn't trap the audio there: recordings can be
transcribed, translated into 60+ languages, and shared anywhere by link or embed
code.

**Where to find it.** The [Mote Chrome Extension](../chrome-extension.md), embedded across Google Docs,
Slides, Classroom, Forms, Gmail, and other education apps.

**Good to know** *(surface when relevant).* Mote for Google Slides does not require
students to have the Chrome extension, and audio added to Slides is also playable on
iPads.
