# Messaging pillars

## Umbrella value proposition

**Mote gives teachers a platform to help every student become a confident,
capable communicator by developing their reading, writing, speaking, and
listening skills alongside the tools and curriculum materials they already use.**

That's the top of the message house. Every pillar below supports it; no
pillar contradicts it.

## The 5 pillars

### 1. Inclusion for every student

**Headline**: Mote puts the same tools in every student's hands,
universal-by-default, not assigned to some.

**Why it matters**: Real inclusion happens when supports are available
to all students by design, not when a few students get singled out with
labeled accommodations. The act of having to ask for an accommodation
is itself a barrier.

**Proof**:

- Mote ships UDL-aligned tools to every student in the class. No
  flagging, no special accounts.
- Students with formal IEPs and 504s get the same tools as everyone
  else; the formal plan documents what they're entitled to, not what
  only they get.
- Backed by Mote's published Tier-1 position in the UDL/MTSS framework
  (see [`../education/udl.md`](../education/udl.md) and
  [`../education/mtss.md`](../education/mtss.md)).

**Audience fit**: Strongest for **teachers** and **SpEd specialists**.
Resonates with **administrators** as an equity story.

### 2. Right inside the tools students already use

**Headline**: Mote keeps students in their work, not bouncing between apps,
by living inside Google Workspace and Chromebooks, and Safari and iCloud on
iOS.

**Why it matters**: Support that sits in a separate app is support a student
has to stop and go get, and every stop is a chance to lose the thread. Mote
works in the places students already are, so the help is one click away and
nothing pulls them out of the task. Its depth in the K-12 Google stack comes
from being purpose-built for it, not from a checkbox integration list.

**Proof**:

- In Google Docs: Read Aloud, Text Prediction, and Mote Writing Review, right
  where students read and write.
- 1-click Read Aloud in Google Forms, including quizzes.
- Mote PDF in Google Drive: a dedicated **Open in Mote PDF** app, and Google
  Drive files are reachable from the Mote PDF web app.
- On iOS: share any webpage or document to Mote from Safari or any app, then
  use Read Aloud, Translation, and the rest.
- Teachers build aligned listening and speaking activities directly in Google
  Forms and Slides, extending, and in some cases replacing, supplemental
  curriculum.
- Google for Education Partner.

**Audience fit**: Strongest for **IT directors** and **administrators**
making platform decisions. Resonates with **teachers** who want support that
does not pull students out of their work.

### 3. Designed for student independence

**Headline**: Mote helps students do their own work (read, write, speak,
revise, create) without teachers having to provide one-on-one support.

**Why it matters**: Teacher time is the scarcest resource in the K-12
classroom. A tool that lets a student self-serve on a barrier (a hard
word, an inaccessible PDF, a writing block) frees the teacher up to
focus on the kids who genuinely need their attention.

**Proof**:

- Read Aloud handles unfamiliar vocabulary without a teacher reading
  along.
- Dictionary explains words inline without breaking the student's
  flow.
- Mote Writing Review explains corrections so students learn from them,
  rather than just accepting them.
- Translation gives English Learner students access to home-language
  explanations.
- Speech to Text lets students who think faster than they type get
  ideas out.
- Voice Recording lets a student answer or think out loud by voice and get a
  clean transcript back, so speaking is another way to work independently.

**Audience fit**: Strongest for **teachers**. Resonates with **SpEd
specialists** as a goal-aligned story.

### 4. Tools for learning, not shortcuts around it

**Headline**: Mote's tools help students engage more deeply with content,
not skip the work.

**Why it matters**: There's growing concern in K-12 about AI tools that
do students' work *for* them. Mote's positioning is the opposite.
Accessibility tools (TTS, STT, Dictionary, Translation) remove barriers
to learning, not the learning itself. Mote Writing Review explains the why
behind corrections; it doesn't just fix the writing.

**Proof**:

- Mote Writing Review *explains* mistakes, doesn't just correct them.
- Read Aloud lets students access content their reading level can't
  yet reach; they still engage with the ideas.
- Speech to Text moves the writing barrier (handwriting, typing,
  spelling) without removing the thinking work.
- Translation provides access without translating away the
  language-development opportunity.
- Mote is **NOT** an AI essay generator.

**Audience fit**: Strongest for **administrators** and **teachers**
worried about AI displacing learning. Resonates with **parents** and
**policy makers** when the messaging reaches them.

### 5. A real partner, not a vendor

**Headline**: Mote ships product updates fast based on what teachers
actually ask for. The team is direct, accessible, and small enough to
care.

**Why it matters**: The K-12 ed-tech market is full of large vendors
who treat schools as accounts. Mote's differentiator at the human level
is the team: direct support from the people who build the product,
fast iteration based on real classroom feedback, and PD sessions that
aren't sales calls.

**Proof**:

- New features ship in weeks, not quarters.
- Customer Success runs real PD sessions, not webinar pitches.
- Schools get a named contact, not a ticket queue.
- 25,000+ schools across 100+ countries: small enough to care, big
  enough to trust.

**Audience fit**: Strongest for **administrators** during the renewal
conversation. Resonates with **teachers** through the responsive-team
story.

## How to use this file

When writing campaign copy, GTM content, or a major piece of marketing:

1. Pick the pillar(s) you're sounding.
2. Use the pillar's headline as your top-level frame.
3. Pull proof points from the proof list. Don't invent new ones.
4. Apply the audience-fit guidance to shape language.

For week-to-week content (blog posts, support docs, individual feature
explainers), pillar awareness is enough. Don't force every piece to map
to a pillar.

For positioning specifics (Geoffrey Moore template, competitive
language) see [`positioning.md`](./positioning.md). For voice rules see
[`voice-and-tone.md`](./voice-and-tone.md).
