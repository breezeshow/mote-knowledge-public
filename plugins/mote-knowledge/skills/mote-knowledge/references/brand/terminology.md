# Terminology

| Term | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|

| Writing review product | **Mote Writing Review** *(a product in its own right; also a button in the Chrome Extension)* | Writing Review (alone), writing review, writing-review |
| Paid tier | **Mote Unlimited** | mote unlimited, Unlimited (alone) |
| Extension type | **Chrome extension** | browser extension, plugin |
| PDF product | **Mote PDF** | MotePDF, motePDF, mote PDF, Mote-PDF |

### Chrome Extension and Mote Sidebar

These are two names for two things, and both are current. The **Mote Chrome
Extension** is the product: the thing an admin deploys and a user installs from
the Chrome Web Store. The **Mote Sidebar** is the panel that extension opens,
holding the student tools. Write the extension when you mean the product or the
deployment, and the Mote Sidebar when you mean the interface a student actually
uses.

Two rules follow:

- **Always write "Mote Sidebar" in full.** A bare "Sidebar" or a bare lowercase
  "sidebar" leaves the reader guessing which of the two you mean.
- **Never call it a toolbar.** Mote reserves "toolbar" for describing Read&Write's
  heavier interface; using it for Mote's own panel blurs that contrast. When a
  generic noun is needed, use **panel**, which is also what Chrome's own platform
  calls it.

## Spelling

Use **en-US (American) spelling** in all Mote copy: *color* not *colour*,
*organize* not *organise*, *center* not *centre*, *license* not *licence*,
*personalize* not *personalise*, and *-ization* not *-isation*. This applies
across every channel, and to quotes rendered in Mote's house style.

**accessibilize** *(verb, en-US -ize)*. Mote's word for what
[Mote PDF](../product/mote-pdf.md) does when it runs OCR on a scanned or
photocopied PDF and turns it into something the whole toolkit can work with. Use
it; do not silently normalize it to "make accessible", which loses the sense that
this is a specific thing the product does to a specific kind of file.

## Inclusive language

Mote serves K-12 students with learning disabilities, reading and
writing challenges, and English Learners. Inclusive terminology
applies across all channels.

### English Learners

- **Primary term: English Learners.** Write it in full.
- **ELL / ELLs is retired.** It was the primary term until 2026-08-05; do not
  use it in new copy, and do not expand English Learners to "English Language
  Learners".
- Leave it as written inside **direct quotations** (customer testimonials, cited
  research) and in the titles of existing assets. Changing a quote to fit house
  style misrepresents the speaker.

### Multilingual learners

- **Additional umbrella term**: *"multilingual learners"*, broader
  than English Learners (includes English-speaking students learning world
  languages).
- **Use when** the content covers both English Learners and English-speakers in
  world-language classes.

### Multilingual learning goals

- **Broadest umbrella**: *"multilingual learning goals"*, describing
  the outcome (developing proficiency in additional languages)
  regardless of starting language.

### Students with learning disabilities

- Lead with the student, not the diagnosis (*"a student with
  dyslexia"*, not *"a dyslexic student"*); person-first language.
- Don't over-medicalise. *"Students who find reading hard"* is
  sometimes better than *"students with reading disabilities"*.
- Avoid *"struggling readers"* as the lead frame; name the barrier
  (decoding, fluency, comprehension) where you can.

### Neurodiverse

- Umbrella term for students with ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dysgraphia,
  and other learning differences.
- The playbook positions Mote as *"AI-powered extension for
  neurodiverse learners"*; acceptable as a short-form descriptor.

## Punctuation rules

- **No em-dashes anywhere.** They're an AI tell. Replace with commas,
  periods, semicolons, or parentheses depending on context.
- **No exclamation marks in the first sentence** of any copy.
- **Maximum one exclamation mark per piece** (and avoid in formal copy
  entirely).
- **No ALL CAPS** for emphasis in body text or headlines.
- **No emoji of any kind. Anywhere. Ever.** This includes decorative
  emoji (smileys, hearts, sparkles), warning glyphs (`⚠`), and the
  semantic-marker emoji often used in tables (`✅`, `❌`, `🎯`, `📝`).
  Use plain text labels instead, like *"Correct" / "Wrong"*,
  *"Do" / "Don't"*, *"Primary" / "Secondary"*, or *"Note:"*, whichever
  fits the table or callout.

## Tier framing (critical)

**Mote tools live universally in Tier 1, and the same tools are used
more intensively at Tier 2 and Tier 3.** What changes across tiers is
*frequency, formality, and depth of use*, not which tools different
students have access to.

This matters because labeled accommodations are themselves a barrier.
The point of UDL is that supports are universally available, so a
student who needs them more never has to be singled out to get them.

| Correct framing | Wrong framing |
|---|---|
| *"Read Aloud is available to every student, and Tier 2/3 students often use it more intensively or formally"* | *"Read Aloud is a Tier 2 accommodation for students with dyslexia"* |
| *"Mote's design point is universal access; the same tools are documented in IEPs and 504s for students who formally need them"* | *"Common 504 accommodations Mote can provide: Read Aloud, Speech to Text"* (implies the tools are gated to some) |
| *"Students who need more intensive support use the same tools, more deeply"* | *"Tier 2 students get Read Aloud + Screen Mask; Tier 3 adds Speech to Text"* (implies different tiers get different tools) |
| *"Mote tools work across all three MTSS tiers, Tier 1 universal, Tier 2 used in targeted small-group support, Tier 3 used in intensive individualised support"* | *"Mote provides Tier 2 and Tier 3 interventions for students who need them"* (drops the Tier 1 universal frame) |

The framing comes from Mote's own published UDL/MTSS position:

> *"Reserving tools for Tier 2 students. Text-to-speech and
> speech-to-text belong in Tier 1 for everyone, not as labeled
> accommodations for some. UDL principles do not turn off at Tier 1."*

## What Mote is NOT (terminology reminder)

- **Not a "voice feedback tool"**: the Chrome Extension is the product.
- **Not a "browser extension"**: the *Mote Chrome Extension* is a *Chrome extension*; Mote
  as a whole is a **platform of products** across Chrome, Apple devices, and the web.
- **Not a "platform" in the vague SaaS sense**: Mote is an *"assistive
  technology software platform"* specifically.

## Products with "don't lead with" guidance

Two Mote products are **active and supported**, but should not be
proactively surfaced in marketing, sales, or outward-facing copy:

- **Mote Classic**: retired product name. Its voice messaging and audio now sit inside the Mote Chrome Extension.
  Active. Described accurately when a user explicitly asks.
- **Mote Translator**: live translation product. Active. Described
  accurately when a user explicitly asks.

When discussing the Mote product line, lead with the Chrome Extension and the
actively-promoted features. Mote Translator remains an accurate part
of the line; it just isn't the recommendation. The accurate
description lives in [`../product/translator.md`](../product/translator.md).

When writing or reviewing copy:

2. Check English Learner / multilingual usage.
3. Confirm scale figures match.
4. Search for em-dashes and replace.
5. Check Tier framing.
6. Make sure you haven't used a banned phrase or descriptor.
