# Voice and tone

## Voice statement

**Mote sounds like a smart colleague.** Short and to the point, plain
language, focused on what the reader actually needs. Not the marketing
department.

That's the test. Anything that fails it is wrong, regardless of how
well-written it is.

## Supporting traits

Five traits flow from the voice statement:

1. **Plain language.** Write so a busy teacher can scan and act. No
   jargon, no acronyms that need expanding mid-paragraph, no
   "platform-speak."
2. **Specific over abstract.** Concrete examples, real numbers, real
   names. *"A student with dyslexia in your 4th-grade class"* beats
   *"students with diverse learning needs."*
3. **Substantive.** Every sentence does work. If a paragraph could be
   cut without losing meaning, cut it.
4. **Honest about trade-offs.** Say what's in beta, what's incomplete,
   what's still being figured out. Don't promise what isn't shipping
   yet.
5. **Light when appropriate.** Warmth comes from being specific and
   useful, not from being chirpy. No forced humour.

## ALWAYS (universal, applies to every use case)

- **Capitalize "Mote"** (never "MOTE" or "mote").
- **Describe the Mote Chrome Extension as a Chrome extension**, never "browser extension"
  or "plugin." Mote as a whole is a platform of products (Chrome Extension, Mote PDF, Mote
  for iOS, and more), not a single extension.
- **Capitalize feature names**: Read Aloud, Text Prediction, Speech to
  Text, Screen Mask, Mote Writing Review, Mote PDF, Translation, Highlighter,
  Dictionary.
- **Use 25,000+ as the school count** (the 20,000 figure floating
  around old files is stale).
- **Be specific about compliance claims** ("TLS v1.2 or higher" not "we
  use encryption").
- **Frame Mote tools as universal in Tier 1, and used more intensively
  at Tier 2 / Tier 3**, never as accommodations *branded for* some
  students. Same tools, used more deeply. (See
  [`terminology.md`](./terminology.md) for the full Tier-framing
  rule.)
- **Use English Learners as the primary term**, written in full. ELLs is
  retired; leave it only inside direct quotations and existing asset titles.

## NEVER (universal, applies to every use case)

### Banned phrases

These don't belong in any channel:

- *"We're excited"*
- *"We can't wait"*
- *"We're thrilled"*
- *"We're proud"*
- *"We're delighted"*
- *"Happy [day]!"*
- *"Hi there!"*
- *"We've been hard at work"*
- *"Game-changer"*
- *"Take to the next level"*
- *"Empower"* (as a verb for the product)
- *"Leverage"* (outside technical contexts)
- *"Seamless / seamlessly"*
- *"Unlock the power of"*
- *"Best-in-class"*
- *"Transforms"* (marketing register; use plain "changes")

### Punctuation and formatting

- **No em-dashes.** They're an AI tell. Replace with commas, periods,
  semicolons, or parentheses depending on context.
- **No exclamation marks in the first sentence** of any copy.
- **No ALL CAPS** words in headlines.
- **No emoji of any kind. Anywhere. Ever.** Includes decorative emoji
  (smileys, hearts, sparkles), warning glyphs (`⚠`), and semantic-marker
  emoji in tables (`✅`, `❌`, `🎯`). Use plain text labels instead,
  like *"Correct" / "Wrong"*, *"Do" / "Don't"*, or *"Note:"*, whichever
  fits.

### Positioning to avoid

- **Never position Mote as "just voice feedback / audio company."** The
  Chrome Extension is the product.
- **Never proactively recommend or lead with Mote Translator.** It is an active,
  supported Mote product. Mote leads with the Chrome Extension and the
  actively-promoted features; Mote Translator is *described accurately when a
  user explicitly asks*, never surfaced unprompted.
- **Never claim Mote replaces teachers**; it augments their work.
- **Never use fear-based messaging** about students falling behind.
- **Never use preachy accessibility messaging**; treat accessibility as
  foundational, not a selling point.
- **Never fabricate compliance claims**; only assert certifications
  Mote actually holds.

## Content defaults

Apply when writing any customer-facing copy. Channel-specific guidance
(Help Scout, sales emails, social, etc.) can override; this file holds
the universal defaults every channel inherits.

- **Sentence case for body and most H1 headlines.** Title Case is
  reserved for H2/H3 in long-form (blog, pillar, landing) content per
  Billy's preference.
- **Feature names in Title Case** wherever they appear (regardless of
  surrounding case).
- **Lead with what the teacher or student can DO differently**, not
  what was built.
- **Structure product descriptions around the three modes**:
  Reading, Writing, Creating.
- **Reference AI only when describing what it specifically enables**,
  not as a buzzword.
- **No raw S3 URLs publicly**; always use `media.mote.com` URLs.

## How to use this file

When writing or reviewing any Mote copy:

1. Read the voice statement.
2. Scan the NEVER list. Anything in your draft on that list comes out.
3. Scan the ALWAYS list. Anything you've gotten wrong gets fixed.
4. Apply the supporting traits as a final pass.

For terminology specifics (capitalization tables, scale figures,
inclusive language), see [`terminology.md`](./terminology.md). For
