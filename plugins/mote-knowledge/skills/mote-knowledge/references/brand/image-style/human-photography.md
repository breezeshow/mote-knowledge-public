# Human photography

## Purpose

Photography of real students (and sometimes teachers and school staff)
in real K-12 classrooms. Used wherever a person needs to carry the
message:

- **Blog post heroes**: most heroes use this style.
- **Pillar-page secondary imagery**: supporting human moments alongside
  the canonical product screenshot.
- **Case-study photos**: real client schools.
- **Lifestyle marketing**: classroom-in-action shots.
- **Social cards with people**: when the post is about a teacher, a
  student, or a school story.

This style does *not* cover product-marketing shots (use
`product-screenshot.md`) or illustration of humans; Mote doesn't do
illustrated humans (see `concept-illustration.md`).

## Subject rules

- **Real students in real classrooms.** Documentary feel: the kind of
  photo that could only have been taken in an actual school. Real desks,
  real bulletin boards, real classroom mess.
- **Chromebooks + headphones are the canonical kit.** Mote's deployment
  context. Students working at Chromebooks with over-ear headphones is
  the recurring visual motif.
- **Diverse representation.** Mote serves K-12 students with learning
  disabilities, reading and writing challenges, and English Learners. Photography
  should reflect that: racial diversity, age range (elementary through
  high-school), visible and invisible disabilities, language
  backgrounds.
- **Authentic moments.** Subjects engaged in real activity: reading,
  writing, listening, discussing, helping each other. Not lined up
  smiling at the camera.
- **No PII without consent.** Student photos require parental consent
  and school approval. Don't ship a real student image unless written
  permission is on file.

## Composition

- **Eye-level or slightly below**, especially for children; never
  shooting down at students.
- **Subject off-center OK.** Rule of thirds, natural negative space.
- **Environmental context**: show the classroom, the desk, the
  Chromebook, the headphones, the bulletin board behind. Avoid the
  floating-on-white-background look.
- **Natural focal depth**: gentle background blur where appropriate
  (other students soft in the background, primary subject sharp), not
  heavy bokeh.

## Treatment / color

- **Natural color grading.** Slight warm tones to align with Mote's
  surface-warm palette, but never an obvious filter.
- **Avoid**: high saturation, heavy contrast, B&W, vintage effects, cool
  blue grading, Instagram-filter looks.
- **Avoid the "ed-tech vendor" aesthetic**: perfectly lit, perfectly
  smiling, perfectly template-diverse subjects. Documentary feel beats
  marketing-bright.

## Sourcing

In order of preference:

1. **Commissioned shoots** in real schools (with consent + approvals).
   Best for case studies, hero campaigns, evergreen content.
2. **Real client schools** sharing existing photography (with consent).
   Often the most authentic option for case studies.
3. **Carefully filtered stock**: only sources that meet the documentary
   bar: Stocksy, Death to Stock, Disabled And Here, Tonl, curated
   Unsplash. Avoid generic iStock / Getty for human photography.

When stock is unavoidable, filter for:

- Documentary feel (not posed-and-lit).
- Real-looking subjects, not models.
- Realistic classroom or learning environment.
- Diverse but not template-diverse (avoid the obvious "one of each").
- Chromebooks + headphones if possible (the Mote kit cues).

## Permissions and PII

- **Students under 18**: parental consent (form on file) + school
  approval before publication on any Mote channel.
- **Teachers and staff**: written consent.
- **Real school names**: school's permission to use in promotional
  context.
- **Identifying student work** (writing samples with names visible):
  same consent chain.

Keep consent records with the asset metadata. When in doubt, blur or
replace.

## Do / don't

**Do**

- A real middle-school student wearing over-ear headphones, focused
  at a Chromebook, with other students soft in the background of a real
  classroom.
- A small group of diverse elementary students wearing headphones
  around shared classroom tables with Chromebooks.
- Carefully filtered stock when commissioning isn't feasible.
- Eye-level or slightly below the subject.

**Don't**

- Lined-up smiling subjects looking at the camera.
- Stock-glossy lighting or over-saturated color grading.
- Generic iStock "diverse students with technology" template shots.
- Photos of identifiable students without parental consent.
- AI-generated humans (uncanny, inconsistent, ethically thin for
  K-12 marketing).
- Heavy filters, B&W, vintage looks, cool blue grading.
- Shooting down at students.

## How to brief a photographer

When commissioning:

> Documentary-style K-12 classroom photography for Mote (assistive
> technology). Subject: **[single student / small group / student-teacher
> interaction]** in a real classroom, working with a Chromebook and
> over-ear headphones. Natural light, warm tones, eye-level or slightly
> below. Documentary feel: capture real moments, not posed shots.
> Minimal post-grading. Diverse representation. Subjects have written
> consent on file.

## How to filter stock

When stock is the only option:

> Search for: real K-12 classroom, student with Chromebook and
> headphones, teacher-student classroom interaction, group of students
> working with laptops, real classroom desks and bulletin boards. Filter
> to: documentary look, natural light, warm tones, authentic moment.
> Reject: posed, over-lit, stock-glossy, template-diverse, B&W,
> over-saturated, filtered.

## Why no AI generation for humans

AI-generated human photography fails Mote's standards in three ways:
technically uncanny output, inconsistent across a campaign (same person
looks different per prompt), and ethically thin for an assistive-tech
brand serving real children. Use real photography or carefully curated
stock.
