# Why It Matters

Why accessible learning matters has two halves: the **need** (how many students are
not being reached) and the **obligation** (the law requires schools to reach them).
This file is the need, in numbers; the obligation (IDEA, Section 504, ESSA) is in
[standards.md](./standards.md). A complete "why" draws on both.

Every figure here is sourced and dated; use these rather than older, unsourced
statistics. Reading scores in particular shift with each release, so check a figure
against the latest data before publishing (this file's `review-interval` is short
for that reason).

## Reading

- **Only 31% of US fourth-graders read at or above the NAEP *Proficient* level,**
  and **40% scored below NAEP *Basic*, the largest share since 2002.**
  *(NAEP 2024 Reading Assessment, NCES.)*
- **Only 30% of US eighth-graders read at or above *Proficient*,** with **33% below
  *Basic*, the largest share ever recorded.** *(NAEP 2024 Reading Assessment, NCES.)*

In plain terms: roughly **seven in ten** US students, in both grade 4 and grade 8,
are not reading proficiently, and scores fell nationally between 2022 and 2024.

## Multilingual learners

- **10.6% of US public-school students (about 5.3 million) were English learners**,
  up from 9.4% a decade earlier. *(NCES, Condition of Education 2024; data for the
  2021-22 school year.)*

## Students with disabilities

- **7.5 million students (15% of all US public-school students) received special
  education under IDEA** in 2022-23. *(NCES, Condition of Education 2024.)*
- **Specific learning disabilities are the single largest category, at 32% of all
  students served under IDEA.** *(NCES, Condition of Education 2024.)*

## Dyslexia

- The **International Dyslexia Association estimates that 15-20% of the population**
  shows some symptoms of dyslexia, and that **about 85% of students with a learning
  disability have their primary difficulty in reading and language.**
  *(IDA, "Dyslexia Basics" fact sheet; no publication year stated.)*

## Writing

- **Only 24% of US eighth-graders wrote at or above the NAEP *Proficient* level,**
  but this is **2011 data, the most recent national writing benchmark that exists.**
  The 2017 NAEP writing results were never released, so there is no current national
  picture of student writing. *(NAEP 2011 Writing Assessment, NCES.)*

## The support works

- A peer-reviewed study of 164 ninth-grade struggling readers found that, after ten
  weeks of **text-to-speech use, reading vocabulary and comprehension improved
  significantly.** *(Park, Takahashi, Roberts & Delise, "Effects of text-to-speech
  software use on the reading proficiency of high school struggling readers,"
  Assistive Technology, 2017.)*

## How to use this file

Use these to open a blog post, frame the "need" section of an RFP, or ground a
district-facing page. **Always carry the source and the year;** an unsourced
statistic is exactly the drift this knowledge base exists to end. When a figure has
a newer release (NAEP reading especially), update it here.
