# Standards & Policy

The US legal and policy backdrop that makes accessible learning a requirement, not
an option, and where Mote fits. This file states the facts; how Mote *helps* with
each is in the linked feature, product, and education files.

## IDEA: Individuals with Disabilities Education Act

IDEA is the federal law guaranteeing students with disabilities a **free appropriate
public education (FAPE)**. Under it, eligible students receive an **Individualized
Education Program (IEP)**, and the IEP team is required to **consider whether a
student needs assistive technology**. Tools like Mote are exactly the kind of
assistive technology an IEP can specify.

## Section 504: Rehabilitation Act

Section 504 prohibits disability discrimination in any program receiving federal
funding, including public schools. A student who has a disability but does not
qualify for an IEP may receive a **504 plan**, a set of accommodations. Schools
can formally name Mote in a 504 plan (Read Aloud, Speech to Text, Translation
are common entries), but Mote's design point is to make these supports
universal: every student has them, not just those with a plan. The 504 documents
what the student is formally entitled to, not what only they get.

## IEPs and 504 plans

Both are formal, legally binding plans for an individual student. An **IEP** (under
IDEA) is for students who need specialized instruction; a **504 plan** is for
students who need accommodations to access the same instruction. Either can name a
specific tool or type of support, which is why Mote is frequently written into a
student's plan as their accommodation.

## ESSA: Every Student Succeeds Act

ESSA is the main federal K-12 education law. It pushes schools toward
**evidence-based** practices and products, and defines four tiers of evidence:
strong, moderate, promising, and "demonstrates a rationale." Districts spending
certain federal funds are expected to choose interventions that meet an ESSA
evidence tier.
