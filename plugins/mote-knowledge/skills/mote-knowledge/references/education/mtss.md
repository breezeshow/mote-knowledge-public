# Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)

**MTSS** is the framework US schools use to organize instruction and intervention
into increasing levels of support, identifying needs early through universal
screening and progress monitoring rather than waiting for students to fail. It has
three tiers:

- **Tier 1, universal.** High-quality core instruction for *every* student;
  reaches roughly **80-90%** of a class.
- **Tier 2, targeted.** Additional, usually small-group, support for the
  **10-15%** who need more.
- **Tier 3, intensive.** Individualised, intensive support for the **5-10%** with
  the most significant needs.

(MTSS is broader than the earlier RTI model; it integrates academic, behavioral,
and social-emotional supports into one framework.) MTSS is now mainstream: by 2023,
**74% of US districts used an MTSS framework**, 71% of them for literacy
specifically. *(Panorama Education, State of MTSS, 2023.)*

## How Mote maps to MTSS

Mote's design point is **universal access**. Every Mote tool is available to
every student in the class through the Chrome Extension; Tier 1 covers the whole
student-facing toolkit. At Tier 2 and Tier 3, students use **the same tools**,
more intensively or formally documented in their intervention plans, IEPs, or
504s. What changes across tiers is *frequency, formality, and depth of use*,
not which tools different students have access to.

This matters because labeled accommodations are themselves a barrier. A
student who needs Read Aloud more intensively never has to be singled out to
get it; it's already on their Chromebook because it's on every Chromebook.

## Tier 2: targeted (same tools, used more deliberately)

Tier 2 students use the **same Mote tools** as everyone else, with more
formality and at higher frequency in their small-group intervention plans.
The features that show up most often at Tier 2 are those that ease common
targeted needs:

- [Webpage Translation](../product/features/webpage-translation.md): structured
  small-group support for English language learners
- [Dictionary](../product/features/dictionary.md): vocabulary scaffolding
- [Screen Mask](../product/features/screen-mask.md): focus intervention for
  attention-related needs
- [Focus Mode](../product/features/focus-mode.md): focus intervention

These are not Tier-2-only tools. They are the same Tier 1 tools, used more
deliberately in a targeted intervention setting.

## Tier 3: intensive individualised (same tools, formally documented)

Tier 3 students use the **same Mote tools** as everyone else, formally
documented as accommodations in their IEPs and 504 plans, and typically used
heavily. The features most often named in Tier 3 plans:

- [Text Prediction](../product/features/text-prediction.md): writing support for
  students with dyslexia, dysgraphia, or motor challenges
- [Speech to Text](../product/features/speech-to-text.md): alternative input for
  students with significant writing barriers
- [Read Aloud](../product/features/read-aloud.md): formally documented
  text-to-speech accommodation
- [Screen Mask](../product/features/screen-mask.md): focus accommodation
- [Focus Mode](../product/features/focus-mode.md): focus accommodation

Again, these are not Tier-3-only tools. They are the same Tier 1 tools, now
documented as the specific accommodations a student is formally entitled to.
The student would have had access to them regardless; the plan documents
their particular need.

## How to use this file

For a task that addresses MTSS (a district-facing page, an RFP, an email to
an intervention team) name the tier and pull the features that map to it,
remembering the framing: **the tools are universal in Tier 1, and used more
intensively or formally at Tier 2/3 — not different tools at different tiers.**
Mote's strong, honest line is that the whole accessibility toolkit is in
every student's hands by default; what changes for Tier 2 or Tier 3 students
is the formality and depth of use, not their access.
